Understanding stock charts is one of the most fundamental skills for any investor. A chart tells the story of a stock's price history — and learning to read that story can help you make better-informed decisions.

## What Is a Stock Chart?

A stock chart is a visual representation of a stock's price over time. The horizontal axis (X) shows time, while the vertical axis (Y) shows price. Each point on the chart represents the stock's price at a specific moment.

## Types of Charts

### Line Chart
The simplest form — connects closing prices over time with a single line. Great for spotting long-term trends at a glance.

### Candlestick Chart
The most popular among traders. Each "candle" shows four data points for a given period:
- **Open** — price when the period started
- **Close** — price when the period ended
- **High** — highest price during the period
- **Low** — lowest price during the period

A **green** (or white) candle means the price closed *higher* than it opened. A **red** (or black) candle means it closed *lower*.

## Key Concepts

### Trend
- **Uptrend** — series of higher highs and higher lows
- **Downtrend** — series of lower highs and lower lows
- **Sideways** — price moves within a range without a clear direction

### Support and Resistance
- **Support** — a price level where the stock tends to stop falling and bounce back up
- **Resistance** — a price level where the stock tends to stop rising and pull back

### Volume
Volume shows how many shares were traded in a period. High volume confirms a price move; low volume suggests it may not last.

## How to Use Stock Market ROI Charts

Our charts support 11 time periods — from **1 Day** (intraday, 5-minute bars) to **15 Years** (monthly view). Use the period buttons at the top of any chart to switch views.

- Use **1D** to see today's intraday price action
- Use **1Y** or **5Y** to assess the long-term trend before making decisions
- The chart color (green/red) always reflects whether the stock is up or down versus the previous close

## Final Thoughts

Charts are a tool, not a crystal ball. Always combine chart analysis with fundamentals — look at earnings, revenue growth, debt levels and valuation before making any investment decision.
